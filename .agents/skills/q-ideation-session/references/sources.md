# Sources and evidence notes

This bibliography supports process guidance. It does not show that any ideation method universally improves creativity, decision quality, or validity.

This list adapts the dated source list of `scientific-brainstorming`, whose authors recorded a research cut-off of **2026-07-23** and checked the links on that date. Quasar has not independently re-verified each link. Verify a source directly before citing it outside this package.

## Generation, blocking, fixation, and selection

- **Diehl and Stroebe (1987)**, "Productivity loss in brainstorming groups: Toward the solution of a riddle," *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 53(3), 497–509. [DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.53.3.497](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1988-01348-001). Four experiments; the fourth supports production blocking as an important cause of loss in interacting groups. Laboratory tasks do not establish superiority for every purpose.
- **Mullen, Johnson, and Salas (1991)**, "Productivity loss in brainstorming groups: A meta-analytic integration," *Basic and Applied Social Psychology* 12(1), 3–23. [DOI 10.1207/s15324834basp1201_1](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1991-24145-001). Reviewed studies generally favored nominal over interacting groups; loss varied with group size and contribution mode.
- **Rietzschel, Nijstad, and Stroebe (2006)**, "Productivity is not enough," *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 42(2), 244–251. [DOI 10.1016/j.jesp.2005.04.005](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103105000600). Nominal groups generated more and more original ideas; selected-idea quality did not differ and selection was not better than chance.
- **Rietzschel, Nijstad, and Stroebe (2010)**, "The selection of creative ideas after individual idea generation," *British Journal of Psychology* 101(1), 47–68. [DOI 10.1348/000712609X414204](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19267959/). Explicit criteria improved selection on the named dimension, with trade-offs in satisfaction — support for explicit criteria, not for a single composite score.
- **Smith, Ward, and Schumacher (1993)**, "Constraining effects of examples in a creative generation task," *Memory & Cognition* 21, 837–845. [DOI 10.3758/BF03202751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8289661/). Exposure to examples increased reproduction of example features: the basis for showing no examples before the independent round.
- **Esser (1998)**, "Alive and Well after 25 Years: A Review of Groupthink Research," *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes* 73(2–3), 116–141. [DOI 10.1006/obhd.1998.2758](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9705799/). The model remains debated; use "groupthink" as a risk prompt, not a diagnosis.

## Structured group methods

- **Van de Ven and Delbecq (1972)**, "The nominal group as a research instrument for exploratory health studies," *American Journal of Public Health* 62(3), 337–342. [DOI 10.2105/AJPH.62.3.337](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1530096/). Early primary account of NGT.
- **Harb et al. (2021)**, NGT scoping review, *Journal of Clinical Epidemiology* 139, 140–148. [DOI 10.1016/j.jclinepi.2021.08.008](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0895435621002535). 57 studies, 30 process decision points: document the choices actually made rather than claiming one invariant protocol.
- **Jünger et al. (2017)**, CREDES guidance, *Palliative Medicine* 31(8), 684–706. [DOI 10.1177/0269216317690685](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28190381/). Conduct and reporting guidance for Delphi; not proof that consensus is correct.
- **RAND (2023)**, *Methodological Guidance for Conducting and Critically Appraising Delphi Panels*. [DOI 10.7249/TLA3082-1](https://doi.org/10.7249/TLA3082-1). Defines Delphi as iterative, anonymous, structured communication under uncertainty and notes inconsistent guidance across variants.
- **EFSA (2014)**, "Guidance on Expert Knowledge Elicitation in Food and Feed Safety Risk Assessment," *EFSA Journal* 12(6):3734. [DOI 10.2903/j.efsa.2014.3734](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2014.3734). Framing, expert selection, uncertainty elicitation, aggregation, documentation.
- **Cooke, Mendel, and Thijs (1988)**, "Calibration and information in expert resolution," *Automatica* 24(1), 87–94. [DOI 10.1016/0005-1098(88)90011-8](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0005109888900118). Do not borrow the "calibration" label without its elicitation, seed questions, and validation.

## Responsible AI

- **Doshi and Hauser (2024)**, "Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content," *Science Advances* 10(28):eadn5290. [DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adn5290](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290). Preregistered experiment with 293 analyzed participants in constrained short-story writing; supports homogenization as a risk hypothesis, not a universal effect.
- **Walters and Wilder (2023)**, "Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT," *Scientific Reports* 13. [DOI 10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5). Model-, prompt-, and date-specific; justifies direct source verification rather than a timeless error rate.
- **European Commission / ERA Forum (2024)**, [Guidelines on the responsible use of generative AI in research](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/news/all-research-and-innovation-news/guidelines-responsible-use-generative-ai-research-developed-european-research-area-forum-2024-03-20_en). Living guidance on transparency, confidentiality, IP, and bias.
- **UNESCO (2023, page updated 2026)**, [Guidance for generative AI in education and research](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research).
- **ICMJE (2026)**, [Use of Artificial Intelligence in Publishing](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence). Humans remain responsible; AI tools are not authors.
- **ALLEA (2023)**, [European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity, revised edition](https://allea.org/portfolio-item/european-code-of-conduct-2023). [DOI 10.26356/ECOC](https://doi.org/10.26356/ECOC).

## Scientific profile only

- **NIH Office of Extramural Research**, [Rigor and Reproducibility in Grant Applications](https://grants.nih.gov/policy-and-compliance/policy-topics/reproducibility/guidance) (page dated 16 October 2024). Grant guidance; it does not validate a candidate or replace a protocol.
- **NIH ORWH**, [Sex as a Biological Variable](https://orwh.od.nih.gov/sex-as-biological-variable) (page updated 17 October 2025), implementing [NOT-OD-15-102](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/not-od-15-102.html). NIH policy scope; other funders differ.
- **Nosek et al. (2018)**, "The preregistration revolution," *PNAS* 115(11), 2600–2606. [DOI 10.1073/pnas.1708274114](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29531091/), and **Nosek et al. (2015)**, "Promoting an open research culture," *Science* 348(6242), 1422–1425. [DOI 10.1126/science.aab2374](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aab2374). Preregistration distinguishes prediction from postdiction; it does not guarantee rigor.
- **WHO**, [Ensuring responsible use of life sciences research](https://www.who.int/activities/ensuring-responsible-use-of-life-sciences-research). Dual-use risk mitigation as shared multi-stakeholder work.
- **U.S. HHS ASPR**, [Dual Use Research of Concern Oversight Policy Framework](https://aspr.hhs.gov/S3/Pages/Dual-Use-Research-of-Concern-Oversight-Policy-Framework.aspx). The upstream authors recorded that federal policy is being revised after the 5 May 2025 executive order; consult the institution and current agency materials rather than a static threshold.
