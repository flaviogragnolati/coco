# Scientific evidence

Load this reference only for scientific or clinical targets or an explicit request for formal scientific appraisal, GRADE, a scientific risk-of-bias instrument, or scientific experimental-design critique. An ordinary vendor benchmark, KPI, survey, or engineering performance claim remains on the quantitative path unless its target is a scientific study or the user requests that formal scientific appraisal. Apply only criteria appropriate to the question and study type.

## Match question, design, and claim

1. Identify the research question, hypothesis, primary outcome, analysis population, and whether the work is exploratory, confirmatory, descriptive, predictive, diagnostic, prognostic, or causal.
2. Test whether the design can answer that question. A study type is not a quality grade by itself; inspect execution, directness, and the claim it is being asked to support.
3. Separate observations from interpretation and association from intervention effect. Require temporality, control of plausible confounding, and an appropriate counterfactual argument for causal language.

## Inspect validity and conduct

- **Internal validity:** allocation, controls, randomization, concealment, blinding, baseline balance, deviations, attrition, confounding, co-interventions, measurement, and selective reporting.
- **External validity:** population, setting, intervention or exposure, comparator, outcome, follow-up, and transportability to the claimed use.
- **Construct validity:** operational definitions, instrument validity and reliability, proxy fit, manipulation checks, and whether the measured construct matches the claim.
- **Statistical conclusion validity:** sample and effective unit, power or precision, multiplicity, model assumptions, effect sizes, intervals, missing data, sensitivity analyses, and predeclared versus post-hoc analysis.
- **Transparency and ethics:** protocol or preregistration, reporting guideline fit, data/code availability where ethically possible, conflicts, approvals, consent, privacy, adverse-event reporting, and deviations from the planned method.

## Interpret bodies of evidence

- Distinguish experimental, observational, mechanistic, qualitative, and synthesis evidence by what each can establish; never let the label replace quality within the design.
- Inspect replication, independent convergence, consistency, directness, precision, publication bias, heterogeneity, and plausible alternative mechanisms.
- For systematic reviews or meta-analyses, inspect protocol, search and inclusion rules, duplicate screening, study-level risk of bias, dependence, heterogeneity, model choice, sensitivity, small-study effects, and whether synthesis is appropriate.
- Apply GRADE only when a defined question and body of evidence fit that framework. Make every downgrade or upgrade factor explicit; do not export its categories as a universal source hierarchy.
- Apply a named risk-of-bias instrument only to the study design and version it supports. Report domain judgments and evidence, not a decontextualized total score.
- Use clinical instruments and reporting guidelines only when applicable to the target; absence of an irrelevant checklist item is not a defect.

## Preserve professional limits

This diagnostic does not replace formal peer review, a systematic-review team, statistical consultation, domain-specialist review, ethics review, regulatory review, or medical or legal advice. For a consequential clinical or regulatory decision, state the exact expertise and evidence not available. Use `insufficient_evidence` or a material limitation instead of certification when the review cannot sustain the requested conclusion.

Complete the scientific branch when design-to-claim fit, applicable validity domains, statistical interpretation, synthesis limits, conflicts, ethics, and specialist coverage are explicit, and the overall conclusion remains no stronger than the inspected evidence.
