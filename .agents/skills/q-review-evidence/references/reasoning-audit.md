# Reasoning audit

Audit the inferential bridge from observations to conclusions. Use fallacy names only as compact labels after identifying the exact premise, missing link, and decision impact.

## Trace the argument

1. Separate observations, measurements, assumptions, interpretations, causal claims, forecasts, and recommendations.
2. Resolve every material premise to an inspected locator or mark it unsupported.
3. State the conclusion in its strongest defensible form and compare it with the wording actually used.
4. Construct the strongest plausible alternative explanation and the evidence that would discriminate between alternatives.
5. Name the condition that would invalidate or materially narrow the conclusion.

## Check common inference failures

| Failure | Evidence signal | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Correlation becomes causation | Association is presented as intervention effect without temporality, control, or confounder handling. | Use associational wording or supply an appropriate causal design. |
| Generalization beyond support | A local sample, environment, version, period, or subgroup becomes a universal claim. | Bound the conclusion to the observed scope and test transportability. |
| Composition or ecological inference | Group behavior is assigned to individuals, or component behavior to the whole system. | Analyze at the level the claim concerns. |
| False dichotomy | Two options are presented as exhaustive without a complete decision space. | Restore material alternatives and mixed cases. |
| Circular support | The conclusion or a source derived from it is reused as independent premise. | Break the cycle with independent evidence. |
| Appeal to authority or novelty | Producer prestige, consensus shorthand, novelty, or an official label substitutes for claim fit. | Inspect the authority's evidence and scope for this claim. |
| Argument from absence | No public report is treated as proof of no event, defect, or effect. | Distinguish no evidence found from evidence of absence and disclose search coverage. |
| Base-rate or denominator neglect | A vivid numerator, percentage, or conditional probability hides the relevant population. | Restore the denominator and applicable base rate. |
| Post hoc or single-cause story | Temporal order or one plausible factor is treated as sufficient cause. | Test confounders, mediators, alternatives, and counterfactual expectations. |
| Moving criteria | Success thresholds, metrics, or explanations change after outcomes are visible. | Recover the predeclared decision rule or label the analysis exploratory. |

For compatibility and engineering claims, require the claimed version, platform, workload, configuration, and failure boundary; evidence in one environment does not establish another. For vendor claims, distinguish authoritative product facts from non-independent comparative interpretation.

Complete this audit when every material inference has visible premises, alternatives, scope, and invalidation conditions, and each recommended rewrite is no stronger than the inspected evidence.
